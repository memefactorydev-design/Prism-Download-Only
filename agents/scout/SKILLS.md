# SKILLS: Scout — Playbooks

## 1. Meta Ad Library mining
1. Go to facebook.com/ads/library → country: the brief's Region (United Kingdom by default) → ad category: All ads.
2. Search each competitor page named in the brief's **Competitors** section; if none are listed, discover the top three for the niche plus regional players around the brief's **Locations & service areas** (search the target keywords from **Search & social** as keywords too).
3. For every active ad, log: start date, format (image/carousel/video/reel), hook (first line/first 3 seconds), offer, CTA, landing URL, number of near-identical variants.
4. **What longevity signals**: an ad running 8+ weeks at an established advertiser is almost certainly profitable — companies don't fund losers for months. Many near-duplicate variants of one concept = a proven concept being scaled. A burst of new ads launched the same day = a fresh testing round; check back in 3 weeks to see which survived (the survivors are the winners).
5. Use "started running" sorting to separate evergreen workhorses from seasonal pushes.
6. Search the live web for the brand name + "ad library" if the direct tool is unavailable, and screenshot/describe what you find with dates.

## 2. Google Ads Transparency Center
1. adstransparency.google.com → region: the brief's Region → search each competitor domain.
2. Log search/display/YouTube creatives, headline patterns (product + offer + location), and which competitors run YouTube vs search-only.
3. Note sitelink/offer language for ASA-safe phrasing examples. Pair with live searches for the brief's top commercial keywords plus its locations to see live auction copy and who bids on brand terms.

## 3. Pinterest & TikTok ad libraries
1. Pinterest: ads.pinterest.com transparency / search the brief's head keyword for promoted pins — Pinterest users plan considered purchases months ahead; log save-worthy formats (moodboards, before/after, "5 ways to…" idea pins).
2. TikTok: library.tiktok.com → the brief's Region → search competitor names and the head keyword. Log which premium brands in the category are present at all (many aren't — a potential first-mover gap) and which creator-style formats (day-in-the-life, behind-the-scenes tours) get sustained runs.

## 4. Hook & angle taxonomy (works for any niche)
Classify every ad you log into one of these, and note sub-variants:
1. **Transformation** — before/after, time-lapse, "from X to Y". Strongest for video whenever the product has a visible result.
2. **Craftsmanship / process** — how it's made, who makes it, the detail that proves quality. Builds price justification for premium positioning.
3. **Price-anchoring / offer** — "from X", cashback, finance, bundles. High volume, risks attracting unqualified leads; finance variants must be compliant.
4. **Lifestyle aspiration** — the product in the customer's life: the moment it makes possible.
5. **Authority/social proof** — reviews, awards, "as featured in", expert-led consultations.
6. **Problem/agitation** — the regret, chaos or horror story the product prevents → the business as the safe choice.
7. **Local/venue** — "visit us in [a location from the brief]", area-specific case studies from the service areas.
Track frequency of each angle across competitors monthly; recommend the business zig where the market zags.

## 5. Creative brief template
For every recommended test, produce:
- **Concept name** & taxonomy angle
- **Evidence**: which competitor ad/pattern inspired it, run-length observed
- **Hook** (first line / first 3s) — write 3 options
- **Visual direction**: exact shots needed (e.g. "macro detail of the flagship product from Overview, filmed at the brief's primary location"), format and aspect ratios (9:16 reel + 1:1 feed)
- **Offer & CTA**: a current offer from the brief's Positioning & USPs and the action named in Goals
- **Primary text & headline** drafts (in the brief's Voice & tone, ASA-safe; finance creative flagged for compliance review)
- **Audience & placement** recommendation, landing page, UTM string
- **Success criteria**: target indicative CPL (scaled to the price band, assumption stated) and minimum spend before judgement

## 6. Ad testing system — 3×3 hook/visual matrix
1. Pick 3 hooks from different taxonomy angles (e.g. transformation, craftsmanship, local) and 3 visuals (before/after reel, founder or expert talking head, beauty pan of the finished product).
2. Build all 9 combinations; identical offer, audience and landing page so only creative varies.
3. Budget evenly; minimum ~50 link clicks or ~2–3× target CPL spent per cell (indicative) before any kill decision; judge on CPL of *qualified* leads, not CTR alone.
4. Kill bottom 5–6 cells, scale top 2–3, then iterate: keep the winning hook, test 3 new visuals against it (or vice versa). One new matrix per month keeps testing velocity honest.
5. Log every cell's result and the learning ("craftsmanship hooks beat offer hooks for lead quality") in a running learnings doc.

## 7. Indicative benchmarks (state as indicative, date every citation)
Benchmarks move with price band, geography and audience narrowness far more than with industry labels — *never quote a single-industry number as if it were this business's*. Before citing anything, search for a current-year benchmark report for the brief's industry and Region, and date it. Use these relationships as sense-checks:
- CPM is driven by audience narrowness and geography, not by what you sell; expect a premium for narrow, affluent or niche targeting.
- CPC ≈ CPM ÷ (10 × CTR%); CPL ≈ CPC ÷ landing-page or form conversion rate. Instant-form CPL runs well below landing-page CPL; qualified CPL runs 2–4× raw CPL.
- Allowable CPL comes from §8, not from a benchmark: a low-ticket product tolerates a CPL that is a small fraction of order value; a high-ticket, considered purchase can justify a CPL that would look absurd in e-commerce.
Never present these as promises; they are sense-check ranges. The business's own historical data always outranks generic benchmarks, and every assumption you make about its price band is stated in the report.

## 8. Funnel maths — lead to sale
Maintain this model and update with the business's real data when provided:
- Map the stages to the brief's Goals: lead → qualified/booked → attended (visit, demo, trial, call) → sale. Drop stages the business doesn't have.
- Stage rates are unknown until the business supplies them — ask, and until then label every rate as an assumption with the figure you used.
- **Allowable CPL = average order value (from Overview's price band) × target marketing cost-of-sale (8–12% is a common band for considered purchases; lower for thin-margin retail) × lead-to-sale rate.** Compute **cost per sale** backwards the same way and show the working.
- Worked shape: N leads at CPL X = spend; × booked rate → appointments; × attended rate → attendances; × close rate → sales; spend ÷ sales = cost per sale, compared with AOV and margin.
- Report blended figures over a window matched to the sales cycle (60–120 days for high-ticket; days for impulse purchases); attribution is slow at the top of the price range.

## 9. Offer intelligence
Map every competitor offer (finance terms, free extras, % off, free consultation/trial) against the current offers in the brief's **Positioning & USPs** — quote only those; never invent an offer for the business. Flag when its offer is being outgunned or, better, when a premium-feeling USP from the brief can be elevated above discount-led competitors.

## 10. Weekly competitor sweep routine
Every week, in order:
1. Meta Ad Library pass on all tracked brands — log new ads, note ads that disappeared (failed tests) and ads passing the 8-week mark (promote to "proven" list).
2. Google Ads Transparency Center pass + 3 live searches for the brief's core commercial terms.
3. Quick Pinterest/TikTok check (10 minutes).
4. Note seasonal/offer shifts (against the brief's Seasonality) and any new entrant advertisers.
5. Output: one-page digest — top 3 observations, 1–2 recommended tests with briefs (§5), any compliance concerns spotted in competitor claims (do not copy non-compliant tactics).
