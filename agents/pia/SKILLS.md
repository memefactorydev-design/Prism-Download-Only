# SKILLS.md — Pia's Pinterest Playbooks

## 1. Board Architecture for the Brief's Niche
1. Run 15–25 boards, each owning one keyword cluster, built from the target keywords in the brief's Search & social section: head terms for each product and service in Overview, style/colour/material modifiers, use-case and occasion boards ("for small spaces", "for entertaining"), planning boards ("ideas", "costs", "how to choose"), and adjacent lifestyle topics the Audience searches.
2. Board titles = exact keywords, never cute names ("[Product] Ideas", not a pun). Board descriptions: 2–3 natural sentences using the head term plus 3–4 related terms and a CTA.
3. Order boards on the profile by commercial priority; set keyword-relevant covers; surface seasonal boards to the top during their window, then demote (don't delete) after.
4. Use board sections to deepen relevance (e.g. a head-term board → With Seating / Small Spaces / Natural Materials, or whatever sub-clusters the keyword research shows).
5. Topical authority matters: the account must look like THE account for the brief's niche. Keep at least 80% of all activity on that niche; no off-topic boards.

## 2. Pin SEO & Keywords
1. Sources for the quarterly keyword map: the brief's Search & social keywords as seeds, Pinterest search-bar autocomplete (type seed terms, harvest suggestions), the related-searches pills on results pages, Pinterest Trends (filtered to the brief's Region), and the ads keyword planner for volume estimates.
2. Optimise every pin's five fields: title (keyword first, ≤60 chars visible), description (100–200 chars, 1–2 key phrases early, natural language, CTA), alt text (literal visual description with one keyword — alt text demonstrably lifts impressions, outbound clicks and profile visits), destination URL (most relevant page, UTM-tagged), board (most relevant board first; cross-save to a second board 3–7 days later).
3. Match keyword intent to destination: inspiration terms → blog/lookbook pages; brand/style terms → product or range pages; planning terms ("[product] cost UK") → guides with lead capture.
4. The account compounds entity recognition: repeatedly covering the same niche topics builds authority — keep keyword themes consistent month to month rather than chasing unrelated trends.
5. Text overlay on images is read by Pinterest's visual search — put the target phrase in the overlay too.

## 3. Fresh Pin Cadence & Production
1. Definition: a fresh pin is a NEW visual, even for an existing URL. Repinning identical images is suppressed; instead create 5–10 different visuals per key URL (different photos, crops, overlays, formats) to hit different visual clusters.
2. Cadence: 1–3 fresh pins daily (7–15/week), scheduled across the day via the native scheduler or a scheduling tool — consistency beats bursts; never dump 10 at once.
3. Format: 2:3 vertical, 1000×1500px, as the strict default. Square/landscape pins lose the majority of their potential reach.
4. Production batching: each completed project or product photoshoot yields a pin kit — 10 straight photo pins, 5 overlay/teaching pins, 3 before/after pins (where the product has a visible result), 2 video pins, 1 multi-image idea-style pin.
5. Refresh strategy: monthly, take the 5 best evergreen URLs and give each one new visual; also update titles/descriptions on decaying pins (metadata updates count toward freshness).
6. Branding: small, consistent logo placement (bottom corner), the typeface and palette from the brief's Visual identity, so pins are recognisably the brand's in the feed.

## 4. Video & Idea-Style Pins
1. Target at least 20–30% of output as video: 6–15 second loops (slow pans, reveals, finish and detail close-ups) and 30–60 second mini-stories (project or product tours, 3-tip lessons).
2. Video specs: 2:3 or 9:16 vertical, text overlay in the first frame (most viewing is muted), branded end-card with CTA.
3. Best-performing video concepts in visual niches: before/after reveals, "three details that make this work", reveals of a hidden feature, side-by-side comparisons in different light, walk-arounds.
4. Multi-page idea-style pins: use for educational series ("How to plan [the project] in 6 steps") — strong for saves and follower growth; include a final page CTA since outbound links are weaker on this format.
5. Ask Nova monthly for trending video formats/hooks from other platforms and translate the structure (not the tone) into brand-appropriate pins.

## 5. Seasonal Content Calendar (publish 8–12 weeks ahead)
1. Build the calendar from the brief's Seasonality section first; overlay it on Pinterest's own lead times, where searches for a moment begin 2–3 months before it.
2. Generic UK skeleton to adapt: **Oct–Nov** pin "new year, new project" planning content for the Boxing Day–January research surge; **Dec–Jan** the January peak — push the Goals action hardest, promote planning guides; **Feb–Mar** spring refresh, Easter and Mother's Day content (pinned from January); **Apr–May** summer and outdoor content (pinned from February–March); **Jun–Aug** pin Christmas-hosting and autumn content (yes, in summer — Pinterest's Christmas searches start in August) plus "order now, ready before Christmas" pins for September publication if the brief's lead times support it; **Sept–Oct** second peak for considered purchases — heavy guide/enquiry promotion; award creative if Harriet or Magnus supply it.
3. Drop or replace any peg the brief's business doesn't share (a wedding supplier, a tax adviser and a garden designer have different years).
4. Check Pinterest Trends (the brief's Region) monthly for rising terms in the niche (e.g. emerging colour or style trends) and produce 2–3 reactive pins within the week while the curve climbs.

## 6. Rich Pins & Technical Setup
1. Keep the website claimed and article/product rich pins validated so pins pull live metadata from the site; re-validate after any site redesign (coordinate with Otto).
2. Ensure the Pinterest tag / Conversions API is firing for PageVisit, Lead and the download or enquiry event named in Goals, gated correctly by the cookie consent banner (GDPR).
3. UTM every destination link (`utm_source=pinterest&utm_medium=organic|paid&utm_campaign=<board-or-campaign>`); reconcile in the site's analytics monthly so reported clicks become reported leads.
4. Add the Pinterest save button to website galleries via Otto so site visitors generate pins for us.

## 7. Pinterest Ads for Lead Generation
1. Campaign ladder: (a) Consideration/traffic campaigns on keyword targets to guides and lookbooks; (b) Conversion campaigns optimised to the Lead event for guide downloads or enquiries; (c) Retargeting (engaged pinners + site visitors) with booking creative and current-offer pins.
2. Targeting: the brief's Region only; layer keywords (25–50 per ad group, exact-ish themes) with interests matched to the Audience section; geo-weight the Locations & service areas for enquiry campaigns; guide-download campaigns can run wider if the business ships or serves remotely.
3. Creative: promote organically proven pins (high save rate) rather than cold creative; refresh every 4–6 weeks before frequency fatigue.
4. Offers in ads must be current and complete (ASA): quote only the offers in Positioning & USPs with material terms; finance ads carry representative-example wording — have copy checked before launch.
5. Measure CPL on downloads and enquiries separately; expect download CPL well below enquiry CPL and judge each on its own target, scaled to the price band (state the assumption). Report monthly.
6. Before scaling spend or entering new keyword clusters, commission Scout for competitor promoted-pin research (what the brief's named competitors — or the top three discovered — are promoting and their creative angles).

## 8. Linking Strategy to Website & Blog
1. Map every board to a primary destination page; flag gaps to Otto ("this cluster has search volume but no landing page — request a guide").
2. Blog-led capture loop: Otto publishes guides for the brief's keywords → you produce 5–10 fresh pins per guide over 6 weeks → guides carry lead-capture forms.
3. Never waste a click: project pins → that project's page; option/colour pins → the matching guide; offer pins → the offer page with the enquiry form above the fold.
4. Quarterly link audit: crawl pin destinations for 404s/redirects (dead links kill pin distribution and waste compounding traffic).

## 9. Measurement & Reporting
1. Monthly dashboard: impressions, saves, outbound clicks, top 10 pins by saves and by clicks, follower growth, engaged audience, site-analytics sessions/leads from Pinterest, ad spend, CPL by campaign.
2. Save rate (saves ÷ impressions) is your creative quality score — replicate the style of anything above account average.
3. Pass insights on winning colours/styles/angles to Isla and Harriet monthly; their audiences usually want the same imagery later.
