# SKILLS.md — Felix's Playbooks

## 1. Facebook Page Optimisation
1. Category: the Facebook categories that best match the brief's Overview (primary + one secondary). Complete every field: every location's address and hours from Locations & service areas, the contact details from the brief, service areas, website, WhatsApp if enabled.
2. CTA button: "Book Now" (or the button that matches the Goals action) → its landing page (UTM-tagged: `utm_source=facebook&utm_medium=page_cta`).
3. Pinned post: best recent transformation or case study + Goals CTA; refresh monthly.
4. Cover: video or image of a flagship project or product, no text overlay (it crops on mobile). Featured section: 3 highlights — Visit Us (if the brief lists a venue) or About, Recent Work, Reviews.
5. Enable and template Messenger auto-replies: instant greeting + "Would you like to [the Goals action]?" with quick replies. Never let a lead sit unanswered overnight without at least the auto-ack.

## 2. Meta Ads Account & Campaign Structure (2025–2026)
1. Default structure — three campaigns, kept deliberately simple so the learning phase exits quickly (~50 conversions/week per ad set is the goal; consolidate, don't fragment):
   - **C1 Lead Gen (prospecting)**: Leads objective, Advantage+ Audience with geo as the only hard constraint (radius/postcode stack around each location in the brief covering a sensible travel time, plus the service areas; national if the business serves remotely). Add age or other constraints only where the Audience section justifies them. Let creative do the targeting.
   - **C2 Retargeting**: manual audiences (see ladder below), Leads or Traffic-to-booking objective.
   - **C3 Local Awareness/Engagement (optional, ≤10% of budget)**: Reach/Engagement to feed C2 with warm audiences and support footfall around events if the brief lists a venue.
2. Advantage+ vs manual: start prospecting on Advantage+ Audience with audience suggestions (lookalike of CRM customers; interest seeds drawn from the brief's Audience and Competitors — adjacent brands, the category's known interests, affluent-area signals where the price band warrants). Only fall back to fully manual if Advantage+ delivers junk leads after the qualifying-question fix (Skill 3) — fix lead quality with form friction and creative before fighting the algorithm.
3. Placements: Advantage+ placements ON, but supply correct ratios — 9:16 video for Reels/Stories, 1:1 and 4:5 for feeds. Vertical video is the native unit in 2026; never run landscape-only.
4. Budgets: prospecting minimum = enough to spend roughly 3–5× target CPL per day so the ad set can learn; never judge an ad before it has spent ~3× target CPL. CBO at campaign level; cost-cap bidding only after 30+ days of stable CPL data.
5. Performance goal: once CRM integration exists, switch to **Conversion Leads** optimisation (CAPI + CRM stage feedback) so Meta optimises for booked Goals actions, not form fills. This is the single biggest lever for lead quality — push for it.

## 3. Lead Forms vs Landing Pages
1. Default to **Instant Forms** for prospecting: 2–3× more leads at similar CPL, 20-second completion. Use the "Higher intent" form type (review screen) for any considered purchase above an impulse price band — volume forms produce tyre-kickers for high-ticket work.
2. Mandatory qualifying questions (multiple choice, not free text): (a) timeline (0–3 / 3–6 / 6–12 months / just researching), (b) budget band with breakpoints scaled to the brief's price band (below typical / typical / above typical order value), (c) location or postcode. Prefill name/email/phone. Conditional logic: "just researching" → guide nurture path, not sales call.
3. Privacy: link the business's privacy policy on every form; add a custom disclaimer for marketing consent if leads will receive email nurture (GDPR — consent must be specific and unbundled).
4. Use **landing pages** for: finance-led offers (full representative terms must be visible), guide downloads feeding email nurture, and retargeting traffic that has already shown intent. A/B the booking page with Otto's input rather than rewriting it yourself.
5. Speed-to-lead: leads must hit the CRM/sales inbox within minutes (native CRM integration or an automation connector). Contact within 15 minutes doubles booking rates — chase the business if leads are going stale.

## 4. Retargeting Ladder (build once, refresh weekly)
1. **Rung 1 — Engagers (180d)**: page/IG engagers, video viewers ≥50%. Serve: tours, client stories, tips. CTA: guide or follow.
2. **Rung 2 — Site visitors (30–90d, excl. leads)**: pixel audiences by page depth (viewed product/service pages vs viewed /contact). Serve: reviews, "what happens next", a walk-through of the venue if the brief lists one. CTA: the Goals action.
3. **Rung 3 — Leads not booked (14–30d)**: CRM custom audience. Serve: founder/expert to-camera "what happens at your consultation", the current offer from the brief, finance message (approved copy only). CTA: pick a time.
4. **Rung 4 — Past clients**: exclusions from all acquisition; separate audience for referral/review asks and cross-sell of the other products & services in Overview.
5. Frequency caps: watch frequency >4/week on Rungs 2–3 — rotate creative or pause. Exclude purchasers and current leads from prospecting.

## 5. Creative Testing Framework
1. Test **concepts before variations**: one concept = one angle (transformation, craft close-up, expert to-camera, client story, offer-led). 3 concepts live at any time, 2–3 variants each, in a dedicated test ad set or Advantage+ creative testing; winners graduate to C1.
2. Naming convention: `YYMM_concept_format_hook_v#` (e.g. `2606_beforeafter_9x16_sundaylight_v2`). Log every result in the learnings sheet: hook, format, CPL, qualified rate, verdict.
3. Proven angles for considered purchases: before/after sliders and videos; "the detail everyone notices" (the feature from the brief's USPs); material or colour stories; real client voice-notes (with permission); "what [the typical order value] actually gets you" (range honesty, within the brief's price band); an invitation with the person who'll be there, if the brief lists a venue.
4. First 3 seconds rule: transformation reveal or bold on-screen question before any branding. Captions burned in (most feed video plays muted).
5. Refresh cadence: assume creative fatigue at 4–8 weeks at typical SME budgets; have next month's concepts briefed to Nova two weeks ahead.

## 6. CPL Benchmarks (derive from the price band — sense-check, don't worship)
1. Never quote a single-industry CPL as the business's. Instead derive it: **allowable qualified CPL = average order value (from the brief's price band) × gross margin × the share of margin you'll spend on acquisition × lead-to-sale rate.** State every input you've assumed; replace with CRM data as soon as it exists.
2. Relationships that hold across niches: Instant Form raw CPL runs well below landing-page CPL; qualified CPL (timeline ≤12 months, budget in band, in area) runs 2–4× raw; cost per booking can be several times qualified CPL and still be strongly profitable at the top of the price range — always anchor to booking→sale rate and AOV from the CRM, with Scout's ROAS model.
3. Alarm thresholds: raw CPL >1.5× target for 5+ days, qualified rate <30%, or booking rate <20% → change one variable (offer, form friction, creative) and document.

## 7. Reviews & Reputation
1. Ask every completed-project client for a Facebook recommendation within 2 weeks of delivery, while delight is peak; provide a direct link and a gentle prompt ("mention the person who looked after you by name").
2. Reply to every review within 24h. Negative reviews: acknowledge, apologise where due, move to private channel, never defensive, never share project details publicly (GDPR). Escalate legal/refund threats to humans.
3. Repurpose 5-star reviews (with written permission) into Rung 2 retargeting creative and monthly organic posts.

## 8. Community & Groups Strategy
1. Identify 10–15 active groups: local community groups across the towns in the brief's Locations & service areas, plus category groups for the brief's niche in its Region. Read each group's promo rules first.
2. Participate as the brand only where allowed; otherwise advise the business owner/team to be helpfully present as individuals. Ratio: 5 genuinely helpful answers (practical advice, "is this quote fair?" guidance — without naming competitors) per 1 soft mention.
3. Run a quarterly "ask the expert" thread or live Q&A where group admins agree. Capture recurring questions as content and ad-angle fuel.
4. Consider an owned group long-term (named after the service area and category) only if posting capacity exists — a dead group is worse than none.

## 9. Posting Cadence & Content Mix (organic)
1. 3–4 posts/week: 1 reveal/before-after or case study, 1 craft/detail or tip Reel (from Nova), 1 social proof (review, client story) or team/venue post, plus 1 seasonal/offer post when relevant.
2. Reels-first: native 9:16 video outperforms stills for reach in 2025–26; stills carousels still convert well for galleries.
3. Best posting windows for a consumer audience: weekday 7–9am and 7–9pm, Saturday morning. Test, don't assume — a B2B brief shifts to working hours.
4. Offer posts: max 1 in 4 posts, quoting only the current offers in Positioning & USPs; finance posts only with pre-approved copy; always "*T&Cs apply" + link.
5. Stories daily where material exists: workshop snippets, delivery days, "this just arrived" — informal, behind the scenes.

## 10. Compliance Pre-Flight (run before every campaign launch)
1. Ad obviously an ad; no misleading edits in before/afters (same angle/lens where claimed).
2. Offer terms stated or one tap away; "free" genuinely free; finance copy pre-approved with representative example.
3. No fabricated reviews, stats, or urgency. No targeting that implies financial status of individuals.
4. Form: privacy policy linked, minimal fields, consent unbundled. Pixel/CAPI events documented in the data register.
5. Correct UTM tags; lead routing tested with a live test lead before scaling spend.
