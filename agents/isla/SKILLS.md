# SKILLS.md — Isla's Instagram Playbooks

## 1. Profile & Grid Strategy
1. Bio formula: what + where + proof + CTA in four short lines — the products & services from Overview | the locations or service areas from the brief | one proof point from Positioning & USPs | the CTA from Goals. Put the head keyword from Search & social in the name field, not just the handle — Instagram search reads it.
2. Link-in-bio: a fast, branded landing page with max four options in Goals priority order (e.g. Book / Download the guide / Visit us, if the brief lists a venue / Latest work). UTM-tag every link.
3. Pin three posts: best-performing reveal Reel, a social-proof post (review/testimonial with consent), and the current offer or seasonal push (only offers the brief lists). Refresh quarterly.
4. Grid rhythm: alternate full reveals, details, process shots and people. Every nine-grid screenful should read as a coherent portfolio — consistent edit/colour grade from the brief's Visual identity, no text-heavy tiles dominating.
5. Highlights as a sales journey: Work, Before/After, Our Process, Visit Us (if the brief lists a venue), Reviews, FAQ, Offers (current only). Audit monthly; keep each under ~15 frames.
6. Cadence: 4–6 feed posts weekly (3–4 Reels, 1–2 carousels/statics). Carousels earn high saves in visual niches — use them for tips and walk-throughs (up to 20 slides; put the payoff early).

## 2. Reels Formats That Perform
1. **Before/after reveal** (the banker wherever the product has a visible result): hook frame of the worst "before" angle, 1–2 second hold, transition (walk-through, hard cut on beat, or door-open wipe), slow pan of the after, end-card with location + CTA. 15–30 seconds.
2. **Process/craft:** how it's made, prepared, fitted or delivered — the workshop, the on-site day, the detail that proves quality. These differentiate the business from competitors who outsource — at least one per week.
3. **Tips:** "3 mistakes people make", "How we do X in 3 steps", "A vs B — the difference". Talking-head or voiceover over real footage; 30–60 seconds; these drive saves and authority.
4. **Tour:** 45–90 second walk-through with voiceover telling the brief→decision→result story. Watch time is the algorithm's no.1 signal; a story arc holds it. Longer 2–3 minute tours are now recommendable and worth testing monthly for accounts that can hold attention.
5. **Client moment:** handover reactions, first use, team-and-client chats (consent required) — these earn sends, weighted far above likes for cold reach.
6. Technical: shoot vertical 9:16 (1080×1920) natively; captions/on-screen text always (assume muted viewing); use trending audio within its first 24–72 hours of rising when tone-appropriate, otherwise low original audio; never export with other apps' watermarks.
7. Use **Trial Reels** to test risky formats on non-followers first; promote winners to the main audience. Get monthly trending-format briefs from Nova and translate them into the brief's register.

## 3. Hooks (first 3 seconds decide everything)
1. Visual hook + text hook together: the most arresting frame of the video plus a 5–9 word overlay.
2. Proven hook patterns in any niche: cost/time transparency ("This took 11 weeks — here's why"), mistake-framing ("The mistake we see in every plan"), contrast ("9 years with the before. 11 weeks to the after."), specificity ("Made for a family who …"), curiosity-with-payoff ("Wait for the end").
3. Never spend the first seconds on logos, fades or establishing context — start mid-action.
4. Keep a hook library: log every Reel's hook and 3-second retention; reuse the top decile structures monthly.

## 4. Hashtag & Instagram SEO Strategy
1. Instagram search is keyword-driven now: put primary keywords from Search & social in the caption's first sentence, the on-screen text and alt text — this outweighs hashtags.
2. Use 3–8 targeted hashtags at the end of the caption: the brand hashtags from the brief's Search & social section if it lists any, otherwise build a set from the target keywords. Mix: niche (product/category terms), community (the niche's UK community tags), local (the towns and areas in Locations & service areas), and one branded.
3. No banned/spammy tags, no 30-tag dumps, no generic "#goals"-style noise.
4. Geotag every project post (area-level, with client consent) and, if the brief lists a venue, tag that location precisely — local discovery feeds the Goals action.
5. Write alt text manually on every post: literal description plus one keyword.

## 5. Stories Sequences for Lead Generation
1. Daily presence (2–4 frames) keeps the account top-of-tray for warm followers — Stories convert; Reels reach.
2. Weekly lead sequence (3–6 frames): hook frame (poll or "this or that" on a real choice) → context frame (project detail, 60% photo / 40% insight) → proof frame (review quote or before/after) → CTA frame with link sticker to the Goals page or "DM us GUIDE".
3. Interactive stickers in most sequences (poll, slider, question box) — replies and taps train ranking and open DM conversations you can convert.
4. Recurring formats: "Behind-the-scenes Monday" (live project progress), "Ask the expert" question box (monthly; answers become content), "Open-door Saturday" (invite walk-ins, tag location — only if the brief lists a venue), offer countdowns for genuine deadlines only (ASA — no fake countdowns).
5. Save every strong sequence to the relevant Highlight so it keeps converting.

## 6. DM Strategy & Automation Suggestions
1. SLA: respond to all DMs within 2 working hours in business hours; speed visibly affects conversion.
2. Conversion script (keep human, never pushy): thank → one qualifying question (project type + rough timeline) → propose the Goals action with a direct link → if not ready, offer the guide and log them for follow-up.
3. Keyword automation (Instagram's native saved replies or a comment-to-DM automation tool): "Comment GUIDE and we'll send it over" on Reels — currently one of the strongest organic lead mechanics on the platform. Disclose automation where it's obvious it's a flow, collect consent before adding anyone to email (GDPR/PECR).
4. Saved replies for the top 10 questions (price band, lead times, areas covered, opening hours if the brief lists a venue) — personalise the first line of every send.
5. Log every qualified DM lead into the CRM with source "Instagram DM" so your KPI reporting reflects reality.

## 7. Collaborations & Influencers
1. Targets: accounts documenting real projects in the brief's niche and service areas (10k–100k engaged followers), trade partners and professionals the business has worked with, suppliers and the creators adjacent to the materials or products the brief names, and local lifestyle accounts across Locations & service areas.
2. Use **Collab posts** for every joint project reveal with partners, professionals and photographers — shared reach into a perfectly matched audience at zero cost.
3. Influencer model for considered purchases: project-based partnerships (their real project, a long documented journey) over one-off sponsored posts; agree deliverables, usage rights for our ads, and exclusivity from direct competitors in writing.
4. Compliance: any gifted or paid arrangement uses the Paid Partnership label and #ad as required by ASA/CMA rules — no exceptions, even for "just gifted" items.
5. Vet partners: audience share in the brief's Region, engagement authenticity (no bought followers), brand-safe history. Ask Scout for a vetting brief on any partner before commitment.
6. Quarterly community play: engage genuinely with 20–30 target accounts (professionals, journalists at the trade and consumer titles that cover the brief's industry, local accounts) to build relationships ahead of press and collab asks.

## 8. Instagram Ads Creative Principles
1. Organic-proven first: boost only Reels/posts that beat account-average saves or watch-through; cold-launching unproven creative wastes budget.
2. Funnel structure (built with Scout's research): reach/views campaigns on hero reveal Reels (broad targeting across the brief's service areas) → lead campaigns (guide download via instant form or site) → retargeting engagers and site visitors with booking creative and genuine offers.
3. Creative rules: vertical 9:16, hook in first 2 seconds, captions burned in, end-card CTA, no more than 20% screen covered in text at any time for comfort, overlays in the brief's Region's English.
4. Lead forms: ask 3 qualifying fields max (location, project timeline, contact); link privacy policy (GDPR); route leads to CRM within the hour.
5. Offer ads: quote only the current offers in Positioning & USPs, verify each is live, include material terms, and use the approved finance wording where finance is involved — every time (ASA and the business's regulated status).
6. Report CPL by campaign monthly; kill creative when frequency >3 or CPL runs 50% over target for two weeks.

## 9. Monthly Cadence & Measurement
1. Monthly volume target: 14–18 Reels, 4–6 carousels, daily Stories, 1 collab post, 1 explicit offer/Goals push per week.
2. Map content to the brief's Seasonality section: its peaks, its quiet months, and the hosting, gifting or planning moments it names.
3. Dashboard: reach (follower/non-follower split), profile visits, website taps, follower growth, saves+sends per post (your two north-star engagement metrics), watch-through on Reels, DM leads, link clicks, Goals actions attributed, ad spend and CPL.
4. Retro discipline: top 5 and bottom 5 posts each month with a one-line hypothesis why; feed conclusions into next month's plan and share with Pia, Harriet and Nova.
5. Quarterly: competitor content audit on the brief's named competitors (or the top three you discover) — who owns storytelling, who owns polish, who owns process — and identify one format gap we can own next quarter.
