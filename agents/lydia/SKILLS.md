# SKILLS.md — Lydia's LinkedIn Playbooks

## 1. The Two-Surface Strategy (founder profile + company page)
1. Personal profiles reach roughly 10× company pages — the founder/MD profile is the flagship; the company page is the proof layer people check afterwards. Budget effort accordingly (60/40).
2. Founder profile optimisation: headline states value not job title ("We [do what the brief's Overview says] the proper way — [region from the brief]" beats "Managing Director"); banner shows the work being done; Featured section holds the best case study, the conversion link from the brief's Goals and one flagship post; About written in first person with real numbers.
3. Company page optimisation: keyword-complete (the brief's target keywords, products & services, region), showcase services, regular behind-the-scenes content so recruitment candidates find a living page.
4. Employee advocacy: when team members are willing, help them post occasionally (a job they're proud of, a problem they solved) — reshares from personal accounts multiply company reach for free. Never compel it.
5. Cadence: founder 2–3 posts/week, company page 2–3 posts/week, never the identical post on both — the page carries the formal version, the founder the candid one.

## 2. Content Pillars & Formats
1. **Decision-led case studies** (the flagship): constraint → options → call → cost band → outcome. Document format (PDF carousel) for depth, single image + story for reach. One per fortnight minimum.
2. **Candour posts:** what jobs cost (scaled to the brief's price band), why you turned work down, what went wrong and the fix, lead-time honesty. Highest engagement pillar — the trade rewards straight talk.
3. **Craft-as-specification:** how the work is done, materials, tolerances, standards — framed as facts a specifier or buyer can rely on, not lifestyle colour. Short native video (subtitled, most watch muted) or photo sequence.
4. **Trade-partnership content:** collaborations with the brief's partner audiences told from their side and tagged (with agreement); "what we need from a brief to quote well" style posts that make the brand easy to work with.
5. **Employer brand:** hiring posts written honestly, new-joiner welcomes, trainee progress. Recruits skilled people and quietly signals a healthy business to partners.
6. Format notes: documents/PDF carousels earn the longest dwell time; native video under 90 seconds; polls sparingly (once a month, real questions only); external links in the first comment, not the post body — LinkedIn suppresses link posts.
7. Monthly **newsletter** (named for the business — "Notes from the [workshop/floor/studio]") once the founder profile passes ~2k followers: one project story, one opinion, one behind-the-scenes note. LinkedIn pushes newsletter invites hard to every connection.

## 3. Audience Building & Outreach (no pitch-slapping)
1. Maintain a living target list of ~50 accounts drawn from the trade and partner audiences in the brief's Audience section (specifiers, resellers, referrers, main contractors, complementary trades and suppliers) within the brief's service areas.
2. Connection request formula: one personal line referencing their actual work, no ask — "Your [named recent project] was superb — the [specific element] especially. Always good to know people doing that standard of work locally." Acceptance target >40%.
3. The 3-touch warm-up: meaningful comment on their post → connection with personal note → value-first DM (share something useful: a detail, a checklist, an honest feasibility offer). Business conversation only when they open the door — or after genuine rapport, a soft "if a [the brand's category] package ever needs a supplier, we should talk properly."
4. Comment strategy: 10+ substantive comments weekly on target-list posts. A specific, technically fluent comment on a target contact's project post is the single highest-ROI activity on this platform — their whole network of peers sees it.
5. Convert online to offline: a quarterly trade evening at a location if the brief lists one (posted as an event), site visits, "bring a brief" feasibility sessions. LinkedIn starts the relationship; the business closes it.

## 4. The Founder Ghostwriting System
1. Capture voice first: 30 minutes of the founder talking (job stories, opinions, gripes) yields a month of posts. Draft from transcripts so the diction is genuinely theirs.
2. Batch weekly: 2–3 drafts in one approval message, each marked with pillar and intended audience. Founder edits or vetoes; never publish unapproved words, and flag anything that asserts an experience they haven't had.
3. Post structure: hook line (fact/tension/number) → 3–6 short paragraphs → one-line takeaway → light invitation. 150–300 words is the sweet spot; documents can carry the long version.
4. Engagement window: the founder (or you, as them, within agreed bounds) replies to every comment in the first 2 hours — early replies double distribution and comments from the founder read as generosity.
5. Keep a swipe file of the founder's best-performing hooks and phrases; their voice should get *more* distinct over time, not more generic.

## 5. Measurement & Compliance
1. Track weekly in a simple sheet: conversations opened, enquiries (trade vs direct), engagers by job title (senior decision-maker > anonymous likes), follower quality. Screenshot analytics monthly for the report.
2. Attribute properly: ask every new trade enquiry how they found the business; "saw your LinkedIn" often arrives months after the posts that earned it — judge pillars on quarters, not weeks.
3. Customer and partner consent in writing before naming projects, budgets or tagging collaborators; partners often have marketing embargoes until completion — always check.
4. GDPR (or local equivalent): connection data and DM contents stay on-platform; export nothing to mailing lists without explicit consent.
5. Review quarterly against the only question that matters: which of the brief's trade and partner audiences now answer our calls because of this channel?
