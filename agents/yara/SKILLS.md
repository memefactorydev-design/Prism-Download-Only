# SKILLS.md — Yara's Playbooks

## 1. Channel Optimisation
1. Channel name = the business name from the brief's Overview; handle to match. Banner: flagship project or product + tagline from Positioning & USPs + "New videos weekly" + Goals CTA; verify links (website and the social profiles from Search & social) in the banner link area.
2. Channel keywords: the target keywords from Search & social plus their "tour", "guide", "cost" and location modifiers.
3. Channel trailer (60–90s) for non-subscribers: who the business is, what the channel shows (tours, real costs, guides), one striking result, Goals CTA. Refresh twice a year.
4. Organise the channel page in intent order: row 1 Tours, row 2 Guides & Cost videos, row 3 Client Stories, row 4 Shorts, row 5 Team (and Venue, if the brief lists one). Playlists titled as searches ("[Head keyword] Tours UK", "[Head keyword] Guides").
5. Upload defaults: the brief's Region's English, location tagged from Locations & service areas where relevant, standard description footer (CTA, locations, contact details from the brief, socials), end screen template (1 video, 1 playlist, subscribe), info cards at natural mention points.

## 2. Video SEO — Titles, Descriptions, Chapters
1. Research before scripting: YouTube search autocomplete + keyword-tool term checks + Otto's keyword data, seeded from Search & social. Target one primary term per video ("[product] ideas", "[product] cost UK", "[product] tour").
2. Title formula: [Keyword] + [specific intrigue or number] — e.g. "[Head keyword] Tour: Every Clever Detail in This [area from the brief] Project". ≤55 characters where feasible; never bait-and-switch.
3. Description: first 150 characters sell the click in search (keyword + promise); then 2–3 paragraphs naturally covering secondary terms; then chapters; then standard CTA footer. Add offer T&Cs lines whenever an offer from the brief is mentioned on camera.
4. Chapters every 60–120s, written as mini-headlines ("The hidden detail", "Where the budget went", "One thing we'd change"). Chapters lift search visibility (key moments) and retention.
5. Hygiene: accurate auto-captions reviewed and corrected (search + accessibility); 5–10 tags including UK/US term pairs where the niche has them; pin a comment with the Goals link within an hour of publishing.
6. Quarterly: re-title/re-thumbnail the 3 videos with the highest impressions but CTR <3%; never change a video that's actively climbing.

## 3. Content Pillars & Calendar
1. **Tours (40%)** — the channel's engine: full walk-throughs of completed work with the expert narrating decisions; honest cost breakdowns scaled to the brief's price band ("where the money went"); tours of the venue, ranges or product lines if the brief lists them. Film every completed project worth showing — the delivery pipeline is the content pipeline.
2. **Client stories (20%)** — before/during/after arcs in the client's own words; the first-contact-to-delivery journey filmed across milestones. Strongest trust asset; requires signed consent before a frame is published.
3. **Guides (25%)** — evergreen search plays built from the target keywords: how to choose, A vs B comparisons, planning steps, "how much does [the product] cost in the UK" (give honest ranges from the price band — this video will be a top performer), and the common-mistakes video.
4. **Shorts (15%)** — see Skill 6. Plus occasional team/workshop films (meet the team, inside the process) to humanise the brand.
5. Cadence: 1 long-form/week (8–12 min sweet spot for considered purchases), 2–3 Shorts/week. Batch-film: one project visit should yield 1 tour, 1 client story segment, 3–6 Shorts and B-roll for guides.
6. Seasonal pegs from the brief's Seasonality section: for most UK consumer brands that means a January planning rush (the biggest search month), a spring push, September nesting, and November–December hosting/gifting plus any offer windows (approved copy only) — adapt to whatever the brief actually names.

## 4. Thumbnails & Hooks
1. Thumbnail rules: one focal subject (the hero shot or a human face reacting naturally), 2–4 words max ("Worth it?", "Before → After"), high contrast, readable at 120px wide, the typeface and colour from the brief's Visual identity so the channel is recognisable in a row. Considered register: no red arrows, no shocked-face clickbait unless Creative licence says otherwise.
2. Produce 2–3 thumbnail options per video; use YouTube's Test & Compare (A/B thumbnails) on every upload and let it pick the winner.
3. Hook structure for long-form (first 30 seconds decides the video): (a) visual payoff tease — the finished result's best 3 seconds; (b) the promise — what the viewer will learn/see; (c) credibility — "we designed and made this"; (d) straight into content. No animated logo intros, no "welcome back to the channel" preamble.
4. Re-hook every 60–90 seconds: tease what's coming ("wait until you see what's behind this"), pattern-break with a detail close-up, or change location. Read the retention graph after 7 days and mark every cliff in the learnings log.

## 5. Repurposing Long-Form into Shorts
1. From every long-form, extract: the reveal moment, the most surprising cost line, the cleverest detail, the best client quote, one expert tip. Each becomes a Short.
2. Reframe to 9:16 (shoot B-roll in 4K so vertical crops survive), burn in captions, add a text hook in the first second, cut to 15–35s — completion rate beats length in 2026's Shorts algorithm.
3. End Shorts with a soft channel CTA ("Full tour on the channel") and use the related-video link so the Short feeds long-form session time — Shorts→long-form flow is what converts Shorts viewers into subscribers.
4. Schedule Shorts between long-form uploads (e.g. long-form Thursday, Shorts Monday/Saturday). 3–5 high-quality Shorts/week maximum — volume-spam depresses average quality and reach.
5. Share masters with Nova for TikTok/Reels adaptation (she owns those platforms' conventions); never just dump identical files everywhere with watermarks attached.

## 6. Shorts Strategy (native)
1. Native Shorts formats that travel across niches: "3 things your [expert] wishes you knew", hidden-feature reveals, satisfying process or mechanism moments, 15-second before/after, "guess the price" (within the brief's price band), colour or material transformations.
2. Optimise for watch-through: 0.5–1.5s visual hook, no intro, loopable endings (final frame matches first) to harvest replays.
3. Titles still matter for Shorts search ("small-space [product] ideas"); write them as searches, not captions.
4. Judge Shorts on completion rate, replays and channel-page clicks — not raw views. A 200k-view Short that brings zero session time is worth less than a 20k one that feeds the tour videos.

## 7. YouTube Ads for Remarketing
1. Audiences: channel engagers (viewed any video, 90–365d), website visitors via Google Ads tag, CRM-matched lists (Customer Match) of leads not yet booked. Coordinate with Felix so the same person isn't hammered on both platforms — agree combined frequency expectations.
2. Formats: skippable in-stream (15–30s cut-downs of tours/client stories) for warm audiences; in-feed ads promoting the cost-breakdown and tour videos to in-market segments for the brief's category, in the service-area geo only.
3. Creative rule: deliver the brand and the result in the first 5 unskippable seconds; CTA overlay to the Goals page; UTM everything.
4. Offer ads: only the current offers in Positioning & USPs, with T&Cs on screen and in description; finance ads only with approved representative-example copy — regulated, no improvisation.
5. Budget: small and steady — a remarketing layer of roughly 10–20% of total paid spend, not an acquisition engine; report cost per Goals-page click monthly and let Scout judge it against Felix's CPL.

## 8. Comments, Community Tab & Conversion
1. Reply to every substantive comment in the first 48 hours (the algorithm's promotion window rewards early engagement); pin either a value-add comment or the Goals link.
2. Treat comments as research: recurring questions ("do you cover [area]?", "what would this cost?") become videos, FAQ chapters and ad angles. Forward in-area, in-budget commenters' enquiries to sales promptly.
3. Community tab 1–2×/week: project polls ("A or B?"), behind-the-scenes stills, new-video pointers — keeps subscribers warm between uploads.
4. Conversion plumbing: every link UTM-tagged (`utm_source=youtube&utm_medium=video&utm_campaign=<video-slug>`); ask Otto to embed each relevant video on a matching site page so videos earn search traffic twice.

## 9. Measurement & Monthly Audit
1. Weekly per-video review at day 7: CTR, APV, retention cliffs, traffic sources, Goals-page clicks. Log one lesson per video — titles/hooks/formats that worked.
2. Monthly: top search terms reaching the channel (feed to Otto), returning vs new viewers, subs per 1,000 views by pillar, Shorts completion trends. Kill or rework any pillar underperforming for 3 consecutive months.
3. Benchmarks to hold yourself to: CTR 4–8%, 30s retention ≥70%, APV ≥40% (8–12 min videos), ≥1 Goals-page click per 200 long-form views once the channel matures. Flag deviations with a hypothesis, not just the number.
