# SKILLS.md — Nova's Playbooks

## 1. Viral Mechanics — The Non-Negotiables
1. **Hook in the first 1.5 seconds**: open mid-action or on the most arresting frame (reveal tease, bold text claim, unusual sound). Never open on a logo, a slow pan, or someone saying "hey guys". The 2026 algorithms weight watch-through rate above almost everything.
2. **Text hook + visual hook + audio hook** stacked in frame one: e.g. a satisfying mechanism or motion (visual) + "[real cost] of work you'll never see" (text) + a crisp natural sound (audio). Three hooks triple the catch rate.
3. **Retention editing**: a cut, movement, zoom or text change every 1–2 seconds; remove every dead frame; on videos >20s, plant a mid-video re-hook ("the best part's at the end — it's the [feature]").
4. **Loops**: end on the frame you opened with, or cut the final word so the replay completes the sentence. Replays count as completions; loops are free watch time.
5. **Length discipline**: default 7–15s for transformations and satisfying clips (highest completion), 20–35s for explainers, 45–60s only when the story earns it. Shorter + completed beats longer + abandoned.
6. **Text overlays**: ≤8 words per card, high-contrast, within centre-safe zones (clear of captions, buttons, nav bars on all four platforms); always burn in captions for sound-off viewers.
7. **Comment engineering (the honest kind)**: leave one genuinely debatable choice visible (option A vs option B, finish X vs finish Y) — discussion in the first hour lifts distribution. Reply fast; pin the best exchange.

## 2. Proven Viral Formats (the portfolio)
Pick the formats that fit the products & services in the brief's Overview; most businesses can run five or more of these.
1. **Before/after transformation**: 3–5s of honest "before" (handheld, raw) → hard cut or whip-pan on the beat drop → polished "after" glide. The single most reliable format for any product or service with a visible outcome. Variant: before/after held in one continuous camera move, matched frame to frame.
2. **Price reveal / budget breakdown**: "This [product/job] cost [real figure]. Where it went:" → 4–6 text cards over detail shots (one line item per card). Honesty about money is rare at any price band and massively shareable. Use real, approved figures only.
3. **Satisfying process & detail**: the mechanism, the pour, the fit, the finish, the peel, the perfectly aligned edge — whatever the business does with its hands or its machines. Shoot close, slow, with crisp natural audio — this is ASMR for buyers.
4. **Rules & mistakes**: "The 5 rules our [experts] never break", "Why your [product choice] is probably wrong", "The [X] mistake in 9 out of 10 [jobs]". Authority + saveability; pure enquiry-driver.
5. **Hidden features & reveals**: doors opening, compartments, mechanisms, the inside of the thing, "what's behind the panel". Reveal formats are an entire genre — most businesses have endless genuine material.
6. **Guess the price / would you?**: show the result, ask "Guess the price — answer at the end" or "[Option A] or [option B]: which would you choose?" Interactive formats farm completions and comments.
7. **Customer story micro-arc**: 30s — "They lived with this for [N] years" → consultation/planning clip → work timelapse → reveal + genuine customer reaction. Requires consent; the highest-converting format you have.
8. **Expert POV/voiceover**: a real person from the business narrating decisions over B-roll. Builds a recognisable face/voice — original audio that can itself trend.

## 3. Trend-Jacking Safely as a Reputable Brand
1. Daily 20-minute scan: platform trend/creative tools, the For You page of a niche-trained account, and 10 benchmark accounts (the brief's named competitors or the top three discovered if none are listed, plus global leaders in the category). Log trends with: format, sound, peak estimate, angle for this brand.
2. The three-question filter before riding anything: (a) Can we execute it with the brief's products/craft at the centre? (b) Would it look right in front of the brand's best customers? (c) Is it pre-peak? Any "no" → skip.
3. Adapt the **structure** of a trend (audio rhythm, cut pattern, caption format), not its comedy register. A trending "things that just make sense" audio works perfectly over product details; a thirst-trap or rage format never will.
4. Speed protocol: trend spotted → script within 24h → filmed from existing B-roll or the next shoot slot → live within 72h. Past 7 days from peak, drop it.
5. Risk rule: anything touching politics, money hardship, other creators' misfortune, or customer privacy → automatic skip or human sign-off. The downside for a brand with a reputation always exceeds the upside of one video.

## 4. Scripting Templates (Hook / Build / Payoff / CTA)
1. **Transformation (10–15s)** — Hook (0–1.5s): "before" + text "They put up with this for [N] years." Build (2–8s): 2–3 quick process beats. Payoff (8–13s): reveal glide on the beat. CTA (final card): "[Brand] — [the brief's conversion action], link in bio." Loop: end frame = opening angle.
2. **Listicle/rules (25–40s)** — Hook: "3 things your [the brief's expert role] wishes you knew." Build: rules 1–2, each over its proof shot, ≤6s each. Payoff: rule 3 is the most surprising ("save the best for last" holds retention). CTA: "Save this for later."
3. **Price reveal (20–30s)** — Hook: total cost on screen, no context. Build: line-item cards over matching detail shots. Payoff: "And the bit nobody budgets for…" CTA: "Want real numbers for your own? [The brief's conversion action] — link in bio." T&Cs line if any offer is named.
4. **Story arc (45–60s)** — Hook: the human problem, customer's words if consented. Build: planning → work milestones. Payoff: reveal + reaction. CTA: "It started with one [the brief's first step: consultation, call, order, visit]."
5. House rules for all scripts: write the text overlays first (they carry sound-off viewers), one idea per video, the promise of the hook must be paid in full, UK English on every frame unless the brief's Region says otherwise.

## 5. Sound & Music Strategy
1. Use each platform's **commercial music library** only — business accounts cannot legally use mainstream trending tracks on most platforms; a takedown wipes the video's stats. Check licence status before editing, not after.
2. Trending-audio play: find commercially-cleared sounds rising in the niche (platform trend tools); early adoption (before ~30k uses) still carries discovery weight on TikTok and Reels.
3. Build **original audio**: the founder's or expert's voiceover, the signature sounds of the work itself. If an original sound gets reused by others, it becomes free distribution.
4. Match edit to beat: cuts and reveals land on the downbeat; transformation reveals on the drop. Export with audio levels balanced — VO −6dB above music bed.
5. Always design for **sound-off first** (captions + overlays carry the story), sound-on as the bonus layer (ASMR detail, music payoff).

## 6. Cross-Posting Matrix — Reels / TikTok / Shorts / Pinterest Video
1. One concept, four native cuts — never one file with another platform's watermark (watermarked content is down-ranked everywhere). Run only the platforms listed in the brief's Channels section.
   - **Instagram Reels**: polished grade, cleaner typography, caption with substance + 3–5 niche hashtags from the brief's brand set (or built from its keywords); post 4–5/week; reply to comments in hour one; share strong Reels to Stories with a poll/slider.
   - **TikTok**: rawer cut acceptable, slightly faster pacing, lean into VO and trending (cleared) audio, text-on-screen heavier; 4–5/week; SEO matters — say and write the brief's target keywords because TikTok is a search engine for buyers.
   - **YouTube Shorts**: hand the concept and master files to Yara — she owns metadata, scheduling and the Shorts→long-form funnel. Flag which long-form each Short should point to.
   - **Pinterest video (Idea-style Pins)**: the slow burner — the highest-intent platform for anything people plan or save for. Keyword-rich titles from the brief's target keywords, text overlay stating the idea, link to the matching site page/board (architecture per Otto). 3–5/week, heavily recycled from the other platforms' winners; Pinterest content converts for 6–12 months.
2. Stagger publishing: TikTok first (fastest feedback), Reels same day or +1, Shorts per Yara's calendar, Pinterest batched weekly.
3. Per-platform tweaks minimum: native captions re-set (each platform's safe zones differ), platform-appropriate CTA (TikTok: follow/comment; Reels: save/link in bio; Shorts: watch full video; Pinterest: visit site), hashtags/keywords per platform conventions.
4. Winner amplification rule: any video at >3× account-average reach within 72h → notify Felix (Spark Ads/boost candidate, whitelisting) and Yara (long-form expansion candidate), and re-cut a variant within a week while the data is hot.

## 7. Measurement & Iteration
1. Per-video log within 72h: hook type, format, length, sound, completion rate, non-follower reach %, saves, shares, profile visits, link clicks. Tag against the format portfolio (Skill 2).
2. Weekly retro: one keep, one kill, one new bet. Monthly: hit-rate review (target ≥1 in 8 over 3× average) and hook-library refresh (retire bottom-quartile hooks, promote new winners).
3. Funnel checks: profile → link clicks (UTM `utm_source=<platform>&utm_medium=organic_social`) → conversions on the action in the brief's Goals, reported monthly. Reach is the means; the brief's Goals are the end.
4. Honesty check on every analysis: never inflate or estimate numbers in reports — pull actuals or say "data unavailable".
