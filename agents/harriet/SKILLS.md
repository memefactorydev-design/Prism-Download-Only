# SKILLS.md — Harriet's Houzz Playbooks

## 1. Houzz Profile Optimisation
1. Complete every profile field at 100%: business description, services, areas served, awards, certifications, typical job cost (from the brief's price band), and the address and hours of every location in Locations & service areas.
2. Business description structure (first 250 characters are what searchers see): who we are + what we make + where we work + the CTA. Lead with the brief's primary product/service term and its service areas.
3. Services list (be exhaustive — each is a directory filter): list every product and service in the brief's Overview as its own entry, plus the closely related services the business genuinely offers.
4. Areas served: list every priority town and area from the brief's Locations & service areas individually — Houzz local rankings depend on explicit area listing.
5. Cover photo: one hero image refreshed quarterly, 2:3-friendly crop, a finished full-room shot (not a detail).
6. Verify the profile claims for every location and keep NAP (name, address, phone) identical to the website and Google Business Profile — flag mismatches to Otto and Magnus.
7. Re-audit the whole profile on the 1st of each month against the current offers in Positioning & USPs; remove expired offers immediately (ASA risk).

## 2. Project & Photo Upload Strategy
1. Structure the portfolio as **projects** (one per real client job), not loose photos. Aim for 12+ live projects, each with 15–25 images: wide establishing shots first, then the hero element, each zone in turn, close-ups of joins and finishes, hardware, and one lifestyle shot.
2. Title formula: *Style + Room/Project type + Location* — "Hand-Finished Period Renovation in a [area from the brief] Terrace". Location words are critical for Houzz search and Google indexing of Houzz pages.
3. Photo description formula (60–120 words each): one design insight + materials named + location + soft CTA. Vary descriptions; never paste identical text across photos.
4. Tag every photo with style, room, colour and finish keywords. Add **product tags** for the materials, finishes and products the brief names — tagged photos surface in product-related searches and get more saves.
5. Upload cadence: 3–5 new or refreshed photos weekly, every week. Houzz feeds and "new photos" surfaces reward steady cadence over bulk dumps.
6. Keyword targets to rotate through descriptions: the target keywords from the brief's Search & social section, their style and room modifiers, and the location modifiers from Locations & service areas. Sync this list with Otto quarterly.
7. After photography hand-off from a completed project, publish the full project within 14 days while it can be tied to a fresh review.

## 3. Ideabook Strategy
1. Maintain 8–12 public ideabooks, each themed to a real search behaviour derived from the brief's target keywords: a colour or material theme, a room or project type, a style, a period-property theme, a "small space" or "entertaining" use case.
2. Mix our images with non-competitor inspiration (architecture, lighting, tableware) at roughly 60/40 ours/others — pure self-promotion ideabooks perform worse and look salesy.
3. Add 3–5 saves weekly across ideabooks; freshness keeps them surfacing in followers' feeds.
4. Write a 2–3 sentence editorial intro on every ideabook with a keyword and a CTA.
5. Seasonal ideabooks on the brief's Seasonality calendar — e.g. a hosting-season book published early autumn, a "new year, new project" book in late December, an outdoor/summer-living book in spring — using the moments the brief actually names.
6. When a homeowner saves our photo, view their profile; if they're in the service area and actively saving in our category, send one polite, personalised message offering the Goals action — one message only, never chase (GDPR-respectful, anti-spam).

## 4. Review Generation Engine
1. Trigger: 7–10 days after project sign-off, the designer or project lead (not a no-reply email) sends a personal review request with the direct Houzz review link. You draft these for the team.
2. Template structure: thank them, reference one specific project detail, one-click link, note it takes three minutes. Never offer incentives for reviews (against Houzz policy and CAP code).
3. Target velocity: 2–4 new reviews per month, steady — review recency and velocity drive directory ranking and Best of Houzz Service.
4. Respond to every review within 48 hours. Positive: thank + specific detail + welcome back. Negative: acknowledge, apologise for the experience (not admit liability), move to phone/email, then post a brief public note when resolved.
5. Quarterly: mine review text for recurring praise themes ("design process", "attention to detail") and feed exact quotes to Pia and Isla for social proof creative, and to the profile description.
6. Ask past happy clients (12+ months on) once a year for an updated "still love it" comment — longevity reviews are rare and persuasive for a premium brand.

## 5. Houzz Pro Advertising
1. Use Houzz Pro targeted local ads to own the brief's category directory placements in the priority areas from Locations & service areas. Start narrow around the brief's locations (or the core of the service area if it has no premises); expand only when CPL is proven.
2. Before any budget change, request a competitive brief from Scout: which competitors hold sponsored slots in our areas, and at what apparent intensity.
3. Track CPL properly: tag Houzz-sourced enquiries in the CRM; count a lead only when contact details + project intent exist. Report CPL monthly against a target scaled to the brief's price band (state the assumption).
4. Creative: rotate the featured project quarterly; lead with the most-saved photo of the quarter (saves predict click-through).
5. Kill rule: pause any targeting area whose CPL runs 2× target for two consecutive months; reallocate to the peaks named in the brief's Seasonality section when intent is highest.
6. Landing path: ads should land on the Houzz profile only when review count is strong; otherwise drive to the website page for the Goals action — A/B check with Scout twice a year.

## 6. Answering Homeowner Questions (Houzz Advice)
1. Answer 5–10 questions weekly on the topics the brief's products & services cover (design, planning, layout, materials, finishes) — prioritise questions from users in the brief's Region and questions with high follower counts.
2. Answer formula: direct answer in the first sentence → one expert nuance most answers miss → optional example from our work → sign off as a designer at the business. CTA only when genuinely relevant.
3. Never answer structural, planning-permission or building-regs questions definitively — recommend a structural engineer or architect (credibility and liability).
4. Save strong Q&As; repurpose monthly as website FAQ content for Otto and carousel content for Isla.

## 7. Winning Best of Houzz
1. **Service badge** = review volume + rating in the calendar year: front-load review pushes in Q4; ensure every autumn completion is asked before mid-December.
2. **Design badge** = photo saves: identify the top 5 most-saved photos each quarter and create more uploads of that style/angle; ask Pia and Isla to drive traffic to those exact Houzz photos in Q4.
3. January: check badge award immediately; add to profile, website (via Otto), email signatures, and brief Pia/Isla for announcement creative the same week.
4. Display previous-year badges accurately with the year — never imply a current badge we don't hold (ASA).

## 8. Measurement & Reporting
1. Monthly dashboard: profile views, photo views, saves, follower growth, enquiries, bookings, CPL, review count/rating, directory rank for 5 tracked area terms built from the brief's category + locations.
2. Attribute properly: ask every inbound lead "where did you first find us?" via the sales team and reconcile with Houzz dashboard numbers — never report platform-claimed leads as bookings.
3. Quarterly competitor benchmark: photo count, review velocity and badge status for the brief's named competitors — or the top three you discover — plus two local independents in our directory areas.
